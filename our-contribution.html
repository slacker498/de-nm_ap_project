Modifications & Alternative Design

Our Contribution

We revised the original passive RLC circuit model by considering a practical low-frequency active alternative with comparable filtering behaviour while addressing the limitations of large inductors and parasitic effects.

1. Modifications

With the continuous development of devices to be more efficient while reducing their bulky nature, we decided to replace the RLC circuit with a band-pass active filter. This was motivated by the bulky nature of the inductor, parasitic noise introduced by larger inductances, and the need for functionality across a wider frequency range.

Instead of relying on the traditional KVL-based comparison, we compared the second-order transfer functions of the standard RLC series circuit and the active filter topology. We also revised the model diagram from a passive RLC configuration to a band-pass active filter using a Sallen-Key topology.

Key design idea

At low frequencies, an inductor becomes physically large and economically difficult to realize. A band-pass active filter provides an LRC-like response while using op-amp circuits and passive components that are more practical for compact low-frequency systems.

2. Theory

A filter is a device that passes electric signals at certain frequencies while preventing the passage of others. In the lower frequency range, active filters become important because they can reproduce LRC-like performance without physically large inductors.

Active filters use an operational amplifier in combination with resistors and capacitors to provide band-pass or other filtering behaviour at low frequencies. In Laplace analysis, transfer functions are used instead of the usual time-domain response because the transfer function approach yields a cleaner comparison of the filter behavior across systems.

Standard transfer-function comparison
$$\frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}} = H(s)$$

The central idea is that, despite different physical implementations, two circuits can be compared by whether their second-order transfer functions and step responses behave similarly under the same damping conditions.

3. Key Handwritten Proof Steps

The most important analytical step in the handwritten derivation is the reduction of the physical circuit into a second-order differential equation for the charge, observed as follows:

$$Lq'' + Rq' + \frac{q}{C} = V(t)$$

The complementary (homogeneous) part of the solution follows from the characteristic equation:

$$Lr^2 + Rr + \frac{1}{C} = 0$$

The discriminant determines the damping behaviour:

$$\Delta = R^2 - \frac{4L}{C}$$

Depending on the sign of the discriminant, the solution takes one of the standard forms:

  • Underdamped: \(\Delta < 0\), giving oscillatory behaviour with exponential decay.
  • Critically damped: \(\Delta = 0\), giving the fastest non-oscillatory return to equilibrium.
  • Overdamped: \(\Delta > 0\), giving a slower, monotonic return without oscillation.
General solution structure
$$q(t) = e^{-\frac{R}{2L}t}(C_1\cos(\beta t) + C_2\sin(\beta t)) + q_p(t)$$
where $$\beta = \frac{\sqrt{|4L/C - R^2|}}{2L}$$

4. Results

The graphs compare the step-response of the standard RLC series circuit and the Sallen-Key topology across all three damping conditions. The most important observation is that although the circuits are physically different, the step responses are nearly identical when configured to match the same general case.

This confirms our contribution: a practical low-frequency active design can emulate the core dynamic behaviour of the original passive circuit without the limitations associated with large inductors and their parasitic effects.

Conclusion from the comparison

The active low-pass/band-pass filter substitutes the passive inductor-based response for a more practical implementation while preserving the expected damping behaviour in a second-order system.

5. Limitations and Further Research

One crucial limitation discovered was that a direct replacement for an RLC circuit is not always possible. One must consider the intended use case, whether for filtering or surge suppression, as well as the frequency range the circuit is expected to operate within. These factors are not always captured directly by KVL-based or second-order transfer-function models.

In future work, we plan to investigate Antoniou gyrators and other active replacement strategies for inductors without changing the resistor and capacitor values in a series RLC circuit. This would allow direct comparison of the charge response rather than only the voltage response used in the modification analysis.

Further comparisons will also be made for other second-order Sallen-Key topology pass circuits, to determine which configuration offers the most generalised solution for standard RLC circuit behaviour.
